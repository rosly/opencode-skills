# Topic 7 — Empirical evidence for TDD

Retrieved: 2026-10-05. Version anchor: TDD is a stable practice (Beck, late 1990s); the empirical literature spans 1999–2025. All findings below are language-agnostic. No Python/pytest specifics appear.

Core question: what do systematic literature reviews (SLRs), meta-analyses, and industrial case studies actually show about TDD's effects on (a) internal quality, (b) external quality/defects, and (c) productivity, and where do they disagree?

## 0. Scope, method, and one attribution correction

- SOURCE FACT — The evidence base is synthesized repeatedly; reviews disagree on productivity in particular. The 2025 tertiary study states: "Test-driven development (TDD) is a software development technique studied empirically over the last few decades. There are several systematic literature reviews (SLRs) on TDD." ([Uyaguari et al., 2025, *Information and Software Technology*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584925001016)).

The 2016 SLR "The effects of test driven development on internal quality, external quality and productivity" is authored by **Bissi, Neto, and Emer** (Crossref/Semantic Scholar record: [OpenAlex W2281418997](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.infsof.2016.02.004)); Causevic/Sundmark/Punnekkat wrote the separate 2011 review on *adoption barriers* ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5770623)). The two must not be conflated. This file keeps them separate.

## 1. External quality / defects — the most consistent positive result

- SOURCE FACT — The most-cited industrial case study reports a large defect reduction. "The results of the case studies indicate that the pre-release defect density of the four products decreased between 40% and 90% relative to similar projects that did not use the TDD practice." ([Nagappan, Maximilien, Bhat & Williams, 2008, *Empirical Software Engineering*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-008-9062-z); same wording in the [Microsoft Research PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2009/10/Realizing-Quality-Improvement-Through-Test-Driven-Development-Results-and-Experiences-of-Four-Industrial-Teams-nagappan_tdd.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — Per-team breakdown of the same study. "All the teams demonstrated a significant drop in defect density: 40% for the IBM team; 60–90% for the Microsoft teams." ([Nagappan et al., 2008 PDF, p. 296](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2009/10/Realizing-Quality-Improvement-Through-Test-Driven-Development-Results-and-Experiences-of-Four-Industrial-Teams-nagappan_tdd.pdf)).
- SOURCE CLAIM — The teams' productivity cost was self-reported, not measured: "the teams experienced a 15–35% increase in initial development time after adopting TDD" (management estimates; labelled "Subjectively" in the abstract). ([Nagappan et al., 2008](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-008-9062-z)).
- SOURCE FACT — The 2016 SLR reports a majority of studies favouring external quality: "88% of the studies identified a meaningful increase in external software quality" when comparing TDD with Test-Last Development. ([Bissi, Neto & Emer, 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950584916300222); citing record [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.infsof.2016.02.004)).
- SOURCE FACT — The Rafique & Mišić meta-analysis (27 studies) is more measured: "in general, TDD has a small positive effect on quality but little to no discernible effect on productivity." ([Rafique & Mišić, 2013, *IEEE TSE*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6197200); [IEEE Computer Society abstract](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/ts/2013/06/tts2013060835/13rRUyYjK6E)).
- SOURCE FACT — The same meta-analysis finds industrial settings amplify the quality gain: "both the quality improvement and the productivity drop to be much larger in industrial studies in comparison with academic studies." ([Rafique & Mišić, 2013](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6197200)).
- SOURCE CLAIM — The 2022 intern/industrial experiment (19 participants, real customer requirements, ~10k LOC) reports a quantified benefit: "Statistically significant advantage of TDD in terms of external code quality (1.8 fewer bugs) and test quality (5 percentage points higher) than TLD." ([Papis, Grochowski, Subzda & Sijko, 2022, *IEEE TSE*](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c742e89d47cebdaf0747c96970e175e16ea76d2)).
- SOURCE CLAIM — The 2025 embedded-systems study (two experiments plus an ethnographic study) reports: "the external quality of the developed ESs increased when using TDD as compared to a non-TDD approach, while there was not a substantial difference in developers' productivity." ([Cassieri, Esposito, Romano & Scannello, 2025, *Information and Software Technology*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584925001181); DOI [10.1016/j.infsof.2025.107779](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2025.107779)).
- AI INFERENCE: external-quality improvement is the empirical claim with the broadest support across study types (industrial case studies, a meta-analysis, an SLR, and two recent experiments). It is, however, "small" in the pooled estimate and heavily moderated by study setting (Section 4).

## 2. Internal quality (design/structure) — evidence is weaker and contested

- SOURCE FACT — The 2016 SLR counts an internal-quality majority: "Most of the studies (76%) have identified an increase in the internal software quality". ([Bissi, Neto & Emer, 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950584916300222)).
- SOURCE FACT — The earlier, more granular Turhan et al. review is far less positive on internal quality: "Available evidence from the trials suggests that TDD does not have a consistent effect on internal quality." ([Turhan, Layman, Diep, Erdogmus & Shull, 2010, *Making Software* ch. 12](http://hakanerdogmus.net/weblog/wp-content/uploads/tdd-sr-book-chapter.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — Turhan et al.'s tally across 14 internal-quality trials: 5 "better", 2 "worse", 3 "mixed", 4 "inconclusive/no-difference" (9 studies after filtering to the most rigorous L2/L3 tiers: 2 better, 2 worse, 1 mixed, 4 inconclusive). ([Turhan et al., 2010, Table 12-3](http://hakanerdogmus.net/weblog/wp-content/uploads/tdd-sr-book-chapter.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — The 2018 review-of-reviews stresses that TDD's claimed design benefits may not come from test-first ordering: "The claimed benefits of TDD may not be due to its distinctive test-first dynamic, but rather due to the fact that TDD-like processes encourage fine-grained, steady steps that improve focus and flow." ([Fucci, Erdogmus, Turhan, Oivo & Juristo, 2017, *IEEE TSE*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/7592412/)).
- SOURCE FACT — The same 39-professional study found order of writing did not matter: "Sequencing, the order in which test and production code are written, had no important influence." ([Fucci et al., 2017](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/7592412/)).
- AI INFERENCE: the internal-quality claim is where reviews diverge most in *magnitude*: Bissi et al. count 76% positive, while Turhan et al. find no consistent effect. The difference is largely a function of which primary studies are admitted and how "internal quality" is operationalised (design metrics vs. presence of tests).

## 3. Productivity — the central unresolved disagreement

- SOURCE FACT — Bissi et al. (2016) state a clear negative: "TDD yields more benefits than TLD for internal and external software quality, but it results in lower developer productivity than TLD." ([Bissi, Neto & Emer, 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950584916300222)).
- SOURCE FACT — Bissi et al. quantify it: "Overall, about 44% of the studies indicated lower productivity when using TDD compared to TLD." ([Bissi et al., 2016, ACM DL record](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.infsof.2016.02.004)).
- SOURCE FACT — Rafique & Mišić (2013) instead find productivity essentially unchanged in the aggregate: "little to no discernible effect on productivity", with the drop appearing only in a subgroup: "A larger drop of productivity was found in studies where the difference in test effort between the TDD and the control group's process was significant." ([Rafique & Mišić, 2013](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6197200)).
- SOURCE FACT — Turhan et al. (2010) report no consensus and setting-dependent direction: "The available evidence from the trials suggests that TDD does not have a consistent effect on productivity." ([Turhan et al., 2010](http://hakanerdogmus.net/weblog/wp-content/uploads/tdd-sr-book-chapter.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — Turhan et al. add a directional split by setting: "The evidence from controlled experiments suggests an improvement in productivity when TDD is used. ... In the industrial studies, the evidence suggests that TDD yields worse productivity." ([Turhan et al., 2010](http://hakanerdogmus.net/weblog/wp-content/uploads/tdd-sr-book-chapter.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — Turhan et al.'s productivity tally across 25 trials: 10 "better", 9 "worse", 0 "mixed", 6 "inconclusive/no-difference" (15 rigorous studies: 6 better, 5 worse, 4 inconclusive — i.e. split). ([Turhan et al., 2010, Table 12-5](http://hakanerdogmus.net/weblog/wp-content/uploads/tdd-sr-book-chapter.pdf)).
- SOURCE CLAIM — Munir et al. (2014), weighting studies by rigor *and* relevance, conclude quality improves but at a productivity cost: "studies with a high rigor and relevance scores show clear results for improvement in external quality, which seem to come with a loss of productivity." ([Munir, Moayyed & Petersen, 2014, via Lund University record](https://www.lu.se/publikation/63eb9aec-1c81-4e38-a85a-d6c6a8d4e8f0)).
- SOURCE CLAIM — Kollanus (2010), as tabulated by a later review, concluded productivity *degrades* with TDD while quality improves. ([Kollanus 2010, tabulated in Karac & Turhan 2018, Table 1](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~dascalus/Paper_JAMES.pdf)).
- SOURCE CLAIM — One industrial multi-case study found the opposite sign for productivity: "The results obtained showed the TDD developers develop software code with a higher quality rate, and it results in increasing team productivity than NON_TDD developers." ([Bakhtiary, Gandomani & Salajegheh, 2020, *Bulletin of EEI*](https://beei.org/index.php/EEI/article/viewFile/2533/1599)).
- SOURCE CLAIM — One controlled experiment with professionals found *no* significant difference either way: "Experimental results and their selective meta-analysis show no statistically significant differences between test-driven development and iterative test-last" development. ([Pančur & Ciglarič, 2011, *Information and Software Technology*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950584911000346)).
- AI INFERENCE — DISAGREEMENT SUMMARY: on productivity there is no consensus. Direction ranges from "lower" (Bissi 2016; Munir 2014; Kollanus 2010; Turhan's industrial subset) through "no discernible effect" (Rafique & Mišić 2013; Pančur & Ciglarič 2011) to "increased" (Bakhtiary 2020). The sign flips with setting (academic vs industrial) and with whether the comparison control also wrote tests.

## 4. What the reviews say side by side (the disagreement, tabulated by a source)

- SOURCE FACT — Karac & Turhan tabulate the SLR conclusions directly, showing they disagree on productivity while mostly agreeing on quality: Bissi et al. "Improvement / Inconclusive"; Munir et al. "Improvement or no difference / Degradation or no difference"; Rafique & Mišić "Improvement / Inconclusive"; Turhan et al. & Shull et al. "Improvement / Inconclusive"; Kollanus "Improvement / Degradation". ([Karac & Turhan, 2018, *IEEE Software*, Table 1](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~dascalus/Paper_JAMES.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — The same review states the bottom line bluntly: "There's no convincing evidence that TDD consistently fares better than any other development method, at least those methods that are iterative." ([Karac & Turhan, 2018](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~dascalus/Paper_JAMES.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — The 2010 review (Shull et al.) found that the positive external-quality signal is fragile to study-rigor filtering: "the supporting evidence from industrial use and controlled experiments disappears after filtering out the less rigorous studies (i.e., L0 and L1 trials)." ([Turhan et al., 2010, ch. 12](http://hakanerdogmus.net/weblog/wp-content/uploads/tdd-sr-book-chapter.pdf); companion summary [Shull et al., 2010, *IEEE Software*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5604358)).
- AI INFERENCE: quality shows directional agreement at the top line ("improvement" in most reviews) but the *robustness* of that agreement is disputed — Turhan et al. show it weakens under rigor filtering, whereas Munir et al. show it *strengthens* when only high-rigor/high-relevance studies are kept. These two results are not contradictory so much as sensitive to which subset is retained.

## 5. Why the reviews disagree: SLR reliability and study-design confounds

- SOURCE FACT — A 2025 tertiary study of eight TDD SLRs finds the reviews do not even select the same primary studies: "What struck us most was that there are pronounced differences with respect to the primary studies included in the SLRs." ([Uyaguari, Acuña, Castro, Dieste & Juristo, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584925001016)).
- SOURCE FACT — It warns against treating any single SLR as definitive: "The reliability of these studies should not be taken for granted because SLRs are highly dependent on the context and researcher decision-making." ([Uyaguari et al., 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584925001016)).
- SOURCE FACT — A 2020 ESEM study isolates five confound categories: "We identified five categories of factors that directly impact the outcome of studies on TDD." ([Ghafari, Gross, Fucci & Felderer, 2020, ESEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09863)).
- SOURCE FACT — The 2011 adoption review lists seven barriers, several of which explain null/reversed productivity findings: "Our review has identified seven limiting factors viz., increased development time, insufficient TDD experience/knowledge, lack of upfront design, domain and tool specific issues, lack of developer skill in writing test cases, insufficient adherence to TDD protocol, and legacy code." ([Causevic, Sundmark & Punnekkat, 2011, ICST](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5770623)).
- SOURCE FACT — Crucially, observed "TDD" in the field often is not TDD: "developers followed TDD in only 12 percent of the projects that claimed to use it." ([Beller et al. 2015, quoted in Karac & Turhan 2018](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~dascalus/Paper_JAMES.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — A GitHub study of TDD-like repositories echoes this and finds no benefit: only 0.8% of projects "adhered to TDD protocol", with "no difference between the two groups" in commit velocity, bug-fixing commits, or reported issues. ([Borle et al. 2017, summarized in Karac & Turhan 2018](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~dascalus/Paper_JAMES.pdf)).
- SOURCE FACT — The task, not the technique, can dominate outcomes. In an industrial experiment with 24 professionals: "the task complexity significantly obscured the effect of TDD." ([Tosun et al., 2017, *Empirical Software Engineering*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-016-9490-0)).
- SOURCE FACT — Same study, the direction-by-task finding: "the subjects are more productive when they implement TDD on a simple task compared to ITLD, but the productivity drops significantly when applying TDD to a complex brownfield task." ([Tosun et al., 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-016-9490-0)).
- SOURCE FACT — A 12-experiment family found a null-to-negative quality result for novices: "TDD novices achieve a slightly higher code quality with iterative test-last development (i.e., ITL, the reverse approach of TDD) than with TDD." ([Santos et al., 2021, *Empirical Software Engineering*; arXiv:2011.11942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11942)).
- SOURCE FACT — Same family, participant type matters: "The quality-related performance of professionals using TDD drops more than for students." ([Santos et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11942)).
- SOURCE FACT — A later TSE industry experiment (17 professionals) also found ITLD ahead: "the participants produce higher quality code during ITLD ... compared to TDD." ([Tosun, Dieste, Vegas, Pfahl, Rungi & Juristo, 2021, *IEEE TSE*](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/ts/2021/11/08884172/1mq8naMbEC4)).
- SOURCE CLAIM — Pančur & Ciglarič anticipated the artifactual explanation: positive results may come from comparing TDD against a coarse-grained control, not from TDD itself. ([Pančur & Ciglarič speculation, summarized in Karac & Turhan 2018](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~dascalus/Paper_JAMES.pdf)).

## 7. Strongest single piece of evidence

- SOURCE FACT — Karac & Turhan's meta-tabulation is the single most discriminating artifact because it places five SLRs' opposing verdicts in one table from primary sources, and records the productivity column as disagreeing (Inconclusive / Degradation / "Degradation or no difference"). ([Karac & Turhan, 2018, Table 1](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~dascalus/Paper_JAMES.pdf)).
- AI INFERENCE: combined with the 2025 finding that the eight SLRs "included" materially different primary studies ([Uyaguari et al., 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584925001016)), the defensible conclusion is: **external quality is the claim most likely to be real; internal-quality and productivity effects are genuinely unsettled and are dominated by moderator variables (task, setting, rigor, and conformance to TDD)** rather than by a stable property of TDD.
- PLAUSIBLE, NOT VERIFIED: a substantial share of the apparent disagreement may be measurement artifact — specifically, self-reported development time (Nagappan) and uncontrolled test effort in the non-TDD control (Rafique's significant moderator; Fucci's "sequencing had no important influence"). No single source in this topic conclusively apportions the disagreement between true effect heterogeneity and design artifact.

## Open questions

- UNKNOWN: What is the industry-only pooled effect of TDD on productivity when the control also writes unit tests (iterative test-last), rather than waterfall/coarse-grained controls? Rafique & Mišić flag test-effort difference as significant but report no clean industry-only estimate; Munir et al. and Turhan et al. lack the data.
- UNKNOWN: Is the small positive external-quality effect causally attributable to test-first ordering, or to the fine-grained/steady-cycle behaviour that co-occurs with TDD? Fucci et al. (2017) found sequencing unimportant, but this has not been replicated in a large industrial RCT.
- UNKNOWN: Does the rise of AI-assisted code generation change the productivity/quality balance? The 2025 embedded-systems study and the 2025 SLR-reliability study are the most current anchors, but neither isolates AI-assisted TDD.
- UNKNOWN: The exact abstract of Dogša & Batič (2011) could not be retrieved directly; only a third-party one-line TLDR ("TDD developers produced higher quality code that is easier to maintain, although ... a reduction in productivity") was available, which is not a verbatim quotation and is excluded from the evidence lines above.
- Open item (belongs to user/report owner, not resolved here): whether to treat "TDD" in field studies as a fidelity-verified treatment or to accept self-declared TDD, given the 12% and 0.8% conformance findings.
